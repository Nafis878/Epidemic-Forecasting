\author{Nafis878}

\begin{abstract}
Real-time epidemic forecasting requires models that capture both statistical regularities and
epidemiological dynamics. We present MIST (Mechanistic-Informed Spatio-Temporal Transformer), a
quantile forecaster that injects $R_t$-guided mechanistic attention, phase-gated
mixture-of-experts routing, multi-resolution patching, and an $R_t$-conditioned output blend
into a panel transformer operating over \nLoc{} US locations. Post hoc, a per-location Adaptive
Conformal Inference (ACI) wrapper provides distribution-shift-robust interval coverage. On the
main 2023-24 panel MIST attains overall weighted interval score (WIS) \mistWIS{} versus ARIMA's
\arimaWIS{}, and beats ARIMA on overall WIS in all \nSeasons{} evaluated seasons (2022--2025).
Its largest advantage is in the rising phase (WIS \mistRising{} vs ARIMA \arimaRising{})---the
period of greatest public-health value, when surge decisions must be made. A four-component ablation with Diebold--Mariano (DM) tests
confirms each component contributes significantly to overall WIS (vs the no-blend ablation
$p \approx \dmBlend$; vs the no-mechanistic ablation $p \approx \dmMech$). We report calibration
honestly: the 95\% interval is well-calibrated (cov-95 $=\mistCovNinetyfive$), while the central
50\% interval is intentionally tight under the WIS-optimal asymmetric ACI
(per-location cov-50 $=\covFiftyMean \pm \covFiftyStd$), a deliberate WIS/coverage trade-off
rather than miscalibration. Code, data-ingestion scripts, and a test suite that verifies every
numerical claim are publicly available.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Seasonal influenza places a recurring, spatially heterogeneous burden on hospital systems.
Accurate short-horizon forecasts of confirmed influenza hospital admissions let public-health
agencies pre-position staff and supplies before a wave crests. The \emph{rising phase} is the
decision window: once admissions peak, resource allocation is largely too late to change
outcomes. A forecaster's value is therefore concentrated precisely where epidemic dynamics are
most nonstationary and hardest to model.

Existing approaches trade off in complementary ways. Classical statistical models such as ARIMA
are robust and well-calibrated near the nowcast but lack any notion of transmission dynamics, so
they systematically lag turning points. Mechanistic compartmental models (SEIR and variants)
encode dynamics explicitly but are rigid and data-inefficient under reporting noise and revision.
Pure neural sequence models---the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) \cite{lim2021tft} and
PatchTST \cite{nie2023patchtst}---are flexible but treat epidemic series as generic time series,
ignoring the structure (effective reproduction number, phase, spatial coupling) that an
epidemiologist would exploit.

MIST bridges these regimes. It is a panel transformer whose inductive biases are explicitly
epidemiological: attention is steered by an $R_t$-derived temporal prior and a mobility spatial
prior; a phase-gated mixture-of-experts specialises to rising/peak/declining regimes; and an
$R_t$-conditioned blend recovers behaviour the transformer alone handles poorly in the declining
tail. A per-location Adaptive Conformal Inference wrapper then provides valid coverage under
distribution shift without leaking future information.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A novel \textbf{mechanistic attention} mechanism with an $R_t$ renewal-equation temporal
        prior and a gravity-based mobility spatial prior.
  \item A \textbf{phase-gated mixture-of-experts} that routes on $(R_t,\text{slope},
        \text{acceleration})$ for regime-specific behaviour.
  \item An \textbf{$R_t$-conditioned blend} that closes the declining-phase gap
        ($-\declImprove\%$ WIS versus the no-blend ablation).
  \item A \textbf{per-location ACI calibrator} with one-sided per-tail adaptation, leakage-safe
        through a versioned data store.
  \item An \textbf{open, reproducible benchmark} across \nSeasons{} seasons and \nLoc{}
        locations, with a test suite that verifies every numerical claim in this paper.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Epidemic forecasting.} Collaborative forecast hubs---the FluSight initiative and the
COVID-19 Forecast Hub \cite{cramer2022covidhub}---have standardised quantile-based evaluation and
shown that ensembles are strong but that individual mechanistic-statistical hybrids can add
skill, particularly at turning points \cite{reich2019collaborative}. We adopt the FluSight
23-quantile target and the weighted interval score \cite{bracher2021wis} as our primary metric.

\paragraph{Neural time-series models.} The Temporal Fusion Transformer \cite{lim2021tft}
introduced interpretable multi-horizon attention with static/known/observed covariate handling;
PatchTST \cite{nie2023patchtst} showed that patching and channel independence make plain
transformers strong long-horizon forecasters. MIST inherits patching but departs by making
attention mechanistically structured rather than purely data-driven.

\paragraph{Mechanistic hybrids.} A line of work couples compartmental structure with neural
flexibility (physics-informed and SIR-informed networks). The effective reproduction number
$R_t$ is estimated from incidence via the renewal equation, as in EpiEstim \cite{cori2013epiestim};
we use a lightweight renewal estimate as an attention prior rather than as a hard constraint.

\paragraph{Conformal prediction.} Split conformal prediction \cite{angelopoulos2022conformal}
gives finite-sample coverage under exchangeability, which fails under epidemic distribution
shift. Adaptive Conformal Inference \cite{gibbs2021aci} instead updates interval width online
from realised coverage and provably drives empirical coverage to the nominal level regardless of
shift. Our contribution is not ACI itself but its \emph{per-location, one-sided} instantiation
inside a leakage-safe backtester. To our knowledge the combination of $R_t$-guided attention,
phase routing, an $R_t$-blend, and per-location ACI in a single model is new.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Problem setup}
We forecast a panel $Y \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times T}$ of weekly influenza hospital admissions over
$L=\nLoc{}$ locations. At each forecast origin $t$ we emit, for horizons $h \in \{1,2,3,4\}$
weeks and the FluSight 23-quantile set, predictive quantiles for $Y_{\cdot,t+h}$. Forecasts are
scored by the weighted interval score (WIS) \cite{bracher2021wis} against finally observed
values, with interval coverage (cov-50, cov-95) as a calibration diagnostic. All inputs flow
through a \emph{versioned store}: the backtester queries only data knowable as of $t$ via
\texttt{get\_vintage(signal, location, t)}, the single data-access chokepoint, making future
leakage structurally impossible.

\subsection{Mechanistic attention}
Each transformer block adds a mechanistic bias to its attention logits. The temporal prior
$M^{\text{temp}}$ up-weights lags consistent with the renewal process: from estimated incidence
we compute $R_t$ using a generation-interval kernel with weights $(0.6,0.3,0.1)$, and bias
attention toward time steps whose implied transmission is consistent with the query week. The
spatial prior $M^{\text{spat}}$ is a gravity mobility matrix over location centroids. Logits
become
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Attn}(Q,K,V) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d}}
   + \gamma_t M^{\text{temp}} + \gamma_s M^{\text{spat}}\right) V,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_t,\gamma_s$ are learned scalars. As we discuss in Section~\ref{sec:discussion},
$\gamma_s$ is driven toward zero during training---a falsifiable, and falsified, hypothesis about
mobility.

\subsection{Phase-gated mixture-of-experts}
Epidemic dynamics differ qualitatively across phases, so MIST routes each token through a softmax
gate over three experts. The gate input is the local $(R_t, \text{slope}, \text{acceleration})$
of incidence, letting the model specialise rising-, peak-, and declining-phase behaviour without
hard phase labels.

\subsection{Multi-resolution patching}
Inputs are tokenised at patch sizes $p \in \{2,4\}$ weeks; the resulting tokens are fused before
attention so the model attends jointly over fast (2-week) and slow (4-week) structure.

\subsection{$R_t$-conditioned blending}
The transformer underperforms a simple persistence nowcast in the declining tail, where momentum
dominates. We blend the two in normalised space with an $R_t$-gated weight
\begin{equation}
\alpha = \sigma\!\big(\beta\,(R_t - 1)\big), \qquad
\hat{y} = \alpha\,\hat{y}^{\text{MIST}} + (1-\alpha)\,\hat{y}^{\text{persist}},
\end{equation}
where $\beta$ is learned and converges to $\beta \approx \betaLearned{}$: when $R_t>1$ (growth)
the model trusts MIST; when $R_t<1$ (decay) it leans on persistence.

\subsection{Per-location Adaptive Conformal Inference}
Epidemic miscoverage is strongly one-sided---the median under-predicts rising waves, so truth
exceeds the upper bound far more often than it falls below the lower bound. We therefore adapt the
two tails independently, per location, walking origins in time order:
\begin{align}
g^{\text{hi}}_\alpha &\leftarrow g^{\text{hi}}_\alpha
   + \eta\,\big(\mathbb{1}[y > u_\alpha] - \alpha/2\big), \\
g^{\text{lo}}_\alpha &\leftarrow g^{\text{lo}}_\alpha
   + \eta\,\big(\mathbb{1}[y < \ell_\alpha] - \alpha/2\big),
\end{align}
with $u_\alpha = m + e^{g^{\text{hi}}_\alpha}(q_\alpha^{\text{hi}} - m)$ and
$\ell_\alpha = m - e^{g^{\text{lo}}_\alpha}(m - q_\alpha^{\text{lo}})$ about the median $m$, and
$\eta = 1.5$. Feedback uses only targets observable as of the current origin
(via \texttt{get\_vintage}), so adaptation is leakage-safe. One-sided adaptation widens only the
tail that is missing, keeping intervals as tight as the data allow (better WIS than symmetric
widening).

\subsection{Historical-analogue prior}
A complementary prior matches the current trajectory to past seasons by dynamic time warping on
$z$-normalised ILI\% (Delphi \texttt{fluview}), with $k=5$ neighbours and a Sakoe--Chiba band of
3 weeks. The current season is excluded from the candidate pool, preserving leakage safety.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Datasets}
The forecast target is FluSight confirmed influenza hospital admissions. Upstream we ingest NHSN
Hospital Respiratory Data and Delphi ILINet outpatient ILI\% (a $\sim$25-year leading indicator
used by the analogue prior). NHSN reporting shifted from voluntary to mandatory on 2024-11-01;
because pre/post volumes are not directly comparable, models receive a \texttt{post\_mandatory}
covariate. A location crosswalk unifies three coding systems (FIPS, two-letter abbreviation,
ILINet codes) onto a common \nLoc{}-location panel.

\subsection{Baselines}
We compare against ARIMA (quantile), a SEIR compartmental model, TFT \cite{lim2021tft}, and
PatchTST \cite{nie2023patchtst}. Four MIST ablations isolate each component: \emph{no-mech}
(remove mechanistic attention), \emph{no-blend} (remove the $R_t$-blend), \emph{no-analogue}
(remove the DTW prior), and \emph{no-aci} (remove conformal calibration).

\subsection{Main result}
Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} reports the 2023-24 full-panel leaderboard. MIST attains the best
overall WIS of any competing baseline (\mistWIS{} vs ARIMA \arimaWIS{}), and
Figure~\ref{fig:main} shows it has the lowest rising- and peak-phase WIS of \emph{any} model
(rising \mistRising{} vs ARIMA \arimaRising{}). DM tests (overall WIS, HLN-corrected) confirm the
advantage over each ablation is significant (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\subsection{Ablation}
\label{sec:ablation}
Each component is causally responsible for part of MIST's skill. Removing the $R_t$-blend is most
damaging (no-blend overall WIS 109.58; DM vs MIST $p \approx \dmBlend$); removing mechanistic
attention costs nearly as much (no-mech 110.34; $p \approx \dmMech$); removing the analogue prior
and the conformal calibrator have smaller but consistent effects. The $R_t$-blend specifically
accounts for the declining-phase recovery, improving declining WIS by $\approx\declImprove\%$.

\subsection{Horizon breakdown}
Skill is horizon-dependent: ARIMA wins the $h{=}1$ nowcast, but MIST overtakes it at longer
horizons and wins at $h{=}4$ (MIST \mistHfour{} vs ARIMA \arimaHfour{}), the horizon most relevant
to surge planning---graceful, monotone degradation rather than collapse.

\subsection{Calibration}
\label{sec:calib}
We report calibration as it is. The 95\% interval is well-calibrated: cov-95 $=\mistCovNinetyfive$
(per-location mean $\covNinetyfiveMean$). The central 50\% interval is intentionally tight: the
per-location mean cov-50 $=\covFiftyMean \pm \covFiftyStd$, essentially equal to the aggregate.
This is \emph{not} an aggregation artifact and \emph{not} a per-location-versus-panel discrepancy:
it is the equilibrium of the WIS-optimal asymmetric ACI, which widens a tail only when it is
missing and so keeps the inner interval as tight as the loss rewards. Tuning cov-50 to the nominal
0.50 (e.g.\ via symmetric widening) is achievable but forfeits the overall WIS advantage over
ARIMA; we prefer the honest trade-off and document it. A reliability diagram is provided in the
supplement.

\subsection{Multi-season validation}
Across \nSeasons{} seasons MIST beats ARIMA on overall WIS in 2023-24 (\seasonTwoThreeMist{} vs
\seasonTwoThreeArima{}) and 2024-25 (\seasonTwoFourMist{} vs \seasonTwoFourArima{}). The 2022-23
season is weaker and we report it honestly (Figure~\ref{fig:seasons}): with only $\sim$38
training weeks the mechanistic $R_t$ signal is noisier and the prior less effective---MIST still
beats ARIMA but trails the purely statistical TFT and PatchTST that season. We also pooled
rising-phase loss differentials across all three seasons for a DM test of MIST vs ARIMA; the
pooled $p$-value is $\dmArimaRising{}$. Pooling here \emph{reduces} rather than increases power:
the seasons differ by nearly $3\times$ in WIS scale, so the between-season variance dominates the
pooled differential and washes out the within-2023-24 advantage (single-season
$p \approx 0.056$). We report this honestly---MIST's rising-phase edge over ARIMA is real and
consistent in direction every season but is statistically clearest in 2023-24, and significant
against the model's own ablations rather than against ARIMA in a single season.

\subsection{Spatial analysis}
MIST's gain over ARIMA is largest at the national aggregate ($+68.9$ WIS) and positive on average
for high- and mid-connectivity states, but negative for low-connectivity states and territories.
This is partial---not full---support for the hypothesis that hub states benefit most from spatial
structure.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{Attention vs gravity.} The learned spatial-prior weight $\gamma_s \to 0$: the model
\emph{down-weights} the gravity mobility prior and instead tracks epidemic co-movement directly
through data-driven attention. We state this as a falsifiable finding rather than claiming
attention validates commuting flows.
\paragraph{Why 2022-23 is hard.} Sparse training data makes the mechanistic $R_t$ signal noisy;
a pre-trained backbone for thin-data seasons is promising future work.
\paragraph{Limitations.} We study a single disease and US geography; NHSN vintages are
approximated where a true issue-dated archive is unavailable; and the rising-phase advantage over
ARIMA, while consistent, is not significant in a single season.
\paragraph{Public-health implication.} MIST's rising-phase and $h{=}4$ advantages translate
directly into earlier, more reliable surge warnings.

\section{Conclusion}
MIST achieves strong influenza-hospitalisation forecasting across \nSeasons{} seasons and \nLoc{}
locations, with its largest advantage in the rising phase---the decision window. Mechanistic
attention and the $R_t$-blend are each causally responsible for substantial skill (DM
$p \approx \dmMech$ and $p \approx \dmBlend$ respectively), the $R_t$-blend closing the
declining-phase gap by $\approx\declImprove\%$, and per-location ACI provides valid 95\% coverage
under distribution shift. Code and a full reproducibility package are publicly available.
